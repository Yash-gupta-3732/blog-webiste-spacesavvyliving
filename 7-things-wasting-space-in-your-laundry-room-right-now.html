Does your laundry room feel like a cramped, chaotic closet where things go to be forgotten? Because the laundry room is traditionally a "utility" space, it often becomes the dumping ground for household clutter. From half-empty detergent bottles to random hardware, this clutter drastically shrinks an already tiny room.

If you want your laundry room to feel bigger, cleaner, and more functional, the first step is ruthless decluttering. Here are 7 things that simply don't belong in your laundry room—and how removing them will instantly create more space.

1. Oversized Plastic Detergent Jugs

Those massive, brightly colored plastic jugs take up a massive amount of visual and physical space. They are difficult to lift and look terrible on open shelves.

The fix: Switch to laundry sheets (which take up zero space), or decant liquid detergents into sleek, uniform glass or acrylic pump dispensers.

2. Spare Household Linens

Many people store spare bath towels, beach towels, and bulky blankets in the laundry room cabinets just because that's where they get washed. But they take up prime real estate.

The fix: Move clean linens to the actual rooms where they are used—under the bathroom sink, in the bedroom closet, or in under-bed storage bins.

3. Random Cleaning Supplies (Not Used for Laundry)

Glass cleaner, toilet bowl scrubbers, and heavy-duty floor chemicals often migrate to the laundry room simply because it's a "cleaning" room.

The fix: Only keep laundry-specific products (detergent, stain remover, dryer sheets) in the laundry room. Move bathroom cleaners to the bathroom and floor cleaners to a utility closet or under the kitchen sink.

4. Duplicate Products

Take a look in your cabinets. Do you have three half-empty bottles of the exact same fabric softener? Duplicate products are the #1 cause of clutter in tiny spaces.

The fix: Consolidate liquids into one bottle, throw out the empties, and implement a strict "one in, one out" rule.

5. Giant Floor-Standing Drying Racks

If you have a tiny laundry room, a massive wooden or metal drying rack that sits on the floor will completely block your workflow and make the room feel claustrophobic.

The fix: Get the rack off the floor! Install a wall-mounted accordion drying rack, or a fold-down ceiling rack that utilizes dead space.

6. The Ironing Board

A full-size ironing board leaning against the wall is an eyesore and a tripping hazard. It takes up critical floor space and constantly falls over.

The fix: Hang it on the back of the laundry room door using a simple over-the-door hook, or mount it securely to the wall.

7. Loose Change, Buttons, and Pocket Trash

We all empty our pockets before washing, resulting in a pile of receipts, loose change, screws, and buttons covering the top of the washing machine.

The fix: Keep a small, aesthetic "pocket catch-all" tray or jar on the shelf specifically for this purpose to contain the visual clutter.

By removing these 7 space-wasters and sticking to the rule of keeping only daily laundry essentials visible, you will instantly double your perceived space and make laundry day significantly less stressful!
